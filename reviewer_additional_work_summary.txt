A) Reviewer Point 1: KL balancing parameter sensitivity and uncertainty-method justification
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Additional work completed
- Added/standardized KL-weight sensitivity sweeps across predefined KL weights (e.g., 1e-6 to 1e-2) in the listed evaluation scripts.
- Ensured each KL setting writes case-level outputs to dedicated KL-specific directories (KL_1e-06, KL_1e-05, etc.) for reproducible comparison.
- Extended comparison scripts to aggregate metrics across KL settings and generate cross-KL summary artifacts.
- Added plotting/summary logic to visualize how predictive performance and uncertainty diagnostics vary with KL weight.

Observed sensitivity trends and implications
- PSML:
  - Coverage increases with higher KL weight, but overly large KL weight (especially 1e-2) over-regularizes the learned posterior and effectively collapses it toward the prior.
  - A clear tradeoff appears between high coverage and uncertainty informativeness: KL=1e-2 gives near-full coverage but the width-error correlation weakens substantially and can become negative in later rolling sessions.
  - KL=1e-4 gives the best overall operating point in PSML: reasonably high coverage (~0.7 solar, ~0.85 wind), positive width-error correlation throughout rolling updates, and stable predictive performance (R²/MAE/RMSE) across sessions.
  - Implication: for PSML, moderate KL regularization (1e-4) balances calibration, usable uncertainty ranking, and predictive accuracy better than aggressive regularization.

- HTTF:
  - Coverage also rises as KL weight increases, consistent with stronger pull toward the prior.
  - KL=1e-4 provides better coverage (~0.7 for TS and ~0.1 for TF) but produces negative width-error correlation.
  - KL=1e-5 gives lower coverage (~0.3 TS and ~0.4 TF) but preserves positive width-error correlation for both outputs.
  - Implication: HTTF exposes a stronger calibration-vs-informativeness tradeoff than PSML; KL selection should prioritize intended use (coverage-oriented vs uncertainty-ranking-oriented).

- NASA Battery:
  - Predictive performance metrics are relatively insensitive to KL weight in both static and rolling settings.
  - This indicates KL tuning has stronger influence on uncertainty behavior than on point-prediction skill in this dataset.

Cross-dataset interpretation
- Best-performing KL weight depends on objective: maximizing coverage alone favors larger KL, while preserving informative uncertainty (positive width-error correlation) favors moderate KL.
- A single global KL weight is not optimal across all cases; dataset-specific operating points are justified.

B) Reviewer Point 2: Calibration and reliability of uncertainty intervals
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Additional work completed
- Added/propagated uncertainty reliability metrics in evaluation and/or comparison scripts, including:
  - empirical coverage of prediction intervals,
  - interval-width vs absolute-error association (Spearman correlation) as a usefulness diagnostic.
- Updated case-level and session-level summaries (where rolling) to include these uncertainty metrics.
- Updated comparison scripts to include uncertainty-centric plots/tables alongside standard predictive metrics.

Dataset-specific reliability outcomes
- PSML (static/rolling):
  - Coverage generally improves as KL increases.
  - At very high KL (1e-2), high coverage can come at the cost of weak/negative width-error correlation, especially after multiple rolling updates.
  - KL=1e-4 maintains positive correlation through rolling sessions while retaining good coverage, indicating more reliable uncertainty for operational use.

- HTTF (static):
  - High test performance across all KL weights does not directly translate into consistently strong global calibration/correlation metrics.
  - Given thermocouple-acquired noisy labels/signals, average coverage and width-error correlation are sensitive to noise even when uncertainty visibly widens at physically plausible points (peaks, valleys, sharp transitions).
  - This suggests uncertainty quality should be interpreted with both global summary metrics and local qualitative behavior.

- NASA Battery:
  - Static training: coverage is poor across KL weights; width-error correlation remains positive but modest (~0.15–0.20).
  - Rolling training: coverage is much better than static but tends to decrease with session index; width-error correlation oscillates around zero (slightly positive/negative by session).
  - Implication: data sparsity strongly limits stability and calibration quality for VI uncertainty, especially in static training.

Reliability implications for manuscript claims
- The added diagnostics show uncertainty is not uniformly reliable across datasets and training modes.
- Rolling updates can improve calibration in sparse-data settings (NASA) but do not fully eliminate session-wise variability.
- Reporting both coverage and width-error correlation is necessary to avoid over-interpreting interval quality from coverage alone.

C) Reviewer Point 3: Practical deployment aspects (rolling stability, computational cost)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Additional work completed
- Added/standardized runtime instrumentation in evaluation scripts:
  - training time,
  - inference time,
  - average per-session times for rolling runs,
  - number of executed sessions (rolling workflows).
- Preserved per-session tracking in rolling settings to analyze behavior under repeated updates.
- Included timing/operational fields in metrics exports to support practical deployment discussion.

Observed stability/practicality outcomes and implications
- PSML rolling:
  - Performance metrics remain consistent through rolling sessions at KL=1e-4 while uncertainty metrics stay behaviorally meaningful (positive correlation).
  - This supports the practicality of repeated updates when KL is chosen in a moderate regime.

- NASA Battery rolling:
  - Coverage improvement over static training demonstrates benefit from rolling adaptation.
  - However, the decline in coverage over sessions plus correlation oscillating around zero indicates uncertainty reliability can drift under sparse-data repeated updates.
  - Practical implication: rolling retraining helps but should be paired with session-wise monitoring and fallback thresholds in low-data regimes.

- Across datasets:
  - Since point-performance sensitivity to KL is often mild (especially NASA), operational KL selection should prioritize uncertainty reliability/stability criteria rather than error metrics alone.
